Understood—with `jj` auto-tracking revisions and your Pascal GTX 1070 pinned to `legacy_580`, the code state is solid.

The driver compilation and blank screen issues come down to two underlying system behaviors:

### 1. Why `legacy_580` Recompiled on `cachyos-lto-znver4`

Out-of-tree kernel modules (`nvidia-x11`) are strictly tied to the exact derivation hash of the kernel headers they build against.

- **Lockfile Hash Shifts:** If `flake.lock` in `Experiment` differs by even a single commit from your main working lockfile for `nixpkgs` or `chaotic-nyx`, the `linuxPackages_cachyos-lto-znver4` header hash changes.
- **Cache Coverage:** Chaotic Nyx caches popular kernel targets, but they do not pre-build every permutation of custom LTO kernels (`znver4`) paired with specific out-of-tree driver branches (`legacy_580`). When a lockfile drift occurs or a specific kernel+driver matrix isn't in Cachix, Nix falls back to compiling the module locally against your CPU architecture.

### 2. Why `/run/current-system` Doesn't Show Your Source Path

`/run/current-system` in NixOS is a symlink pointing directly into the Nix store (`/nix/store/<hash>-nixos-system-nixos-...`), not your local repository directory. It will never point to `~/NixOS/Experiment`. To verify which commit built the current generation, run:

nixos-version --json

### 3. Why Niri Blank-Screened (Dual GPU Primary Display Conflict)

Your hardware configuration initializes AMD integrated graphics in stage-1 initrd:

boot.initrd.kernelModules = [ "amdgpu" ];

On a system with a Ryzen 7600X (which has an iGPU) and a GTX 1070:

1. `amdgpu` initializes first in initrd and claims `/dev/dri/card0`.
2. The GTX 1070 initializes second and becomes `/dev/dri/card1`.
3. If your monitors are physically plugged into the GTX 1070, Niri/Wayland attempts to start rendering on `card0` (the iGPU) by default. Because no physical displays are attached to the motherboard outputs, it fails buffer scanout and drops to a black screen.

#### The Fix

Tell Niri and Wayland explicitly to prioritize the Nvidia GPU (`card1`) or force the render device in your UWSM wrapper environment:

# Inside your myUWSM wrapper env or system session variables
env = {
  # Direct wlroots/Wayland to prioritize the dGPU over the iGPU
  "WLR_DRM_DEVICES" = "/dev/dri/by-path/pci-0000:01:00.0-card:/dev/dri/by-path/pci-0000:09:00.0-card";
  "GBM_BACKEND" = "nvidia-drm";
  "__GLX_VENDOR_LIBRARY_NAME" = "nvidia";
};

_(You can check the exact PCI device paths under `/dev/dri/by-path/` to ensure card order remains consistent regardless of boot timing)._
